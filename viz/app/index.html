Corpus

The Manual's chunks and the provisions of the Act and the Regulations are filtered by one set of predicates. Where a predicate belongs to one side only — Part, heading source, page metadata, case law — choosing it leaves the other side out.

Part
Kind

body, annex and landing are the Manual's chunk kinds — a landing page is a legislation mapping, not prose. The rest are what a provision of the legislation is: section, regulation, clause, item, and the two that exist so no text falls out of the corpus.

Heading source

markup is a heading the Manual marked up itself. emphasis is a bold numbered paragraph the chunker promoted — the pipeline's only recorded inference. none is the prose above a page's first heading.

Case law
Structure

Read against a provision too, where it has the same meaning: what it cites, whether anything cites it, whether it holds a table. The three that name a chunk's own fields leave the legislation out.

Page metadata

The Manual states the Registrar's practice under the Trade Marks Act 1995 (Cth). It is not the legislation and does not bind the Registrar's discretion. The legislation here is one compilation of it, as registered on a date shown against each instrument, and a compilation can be behind the law in force. This viewer reads a dated snapshot — check the source page or the Federal Register of Legislation before relying on anything here.
